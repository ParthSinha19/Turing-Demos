Model 1

👁 Teach the AI to See — HAND vs FIST

The model doesn't "understand" hands. It turns each picture into a grid of numbers (a feature vector) and finds the closest matching examples — this is called K-Nearest Neighbours.

Before comparing, every image — whether it's a photo, a webcam capture, or a plain black-and-white mask — is automatically simplified into a centred foreground shape. This stops the model from being thrown off by different backgrounds, colours or camera angles. For best results, use a plain, fairly uniform background behind the hand when uploading photos or using the camera.

Both mini-models you just used follow the exact same recipe as much bigger real-world AI systems:

🚗 Tesla-style perception

Uses millions of real driving images, huge neural networks, and enormous amounts of computing power — not 20 hand-drawn examples and a nearest-neighbour lookup.

💬 ChatGPT

Uses a completely different architecture (a huge transformer trained on vast amounts of text) — but the underlying idea is related: learn patterns from lots of data, then predict.

The goal today wasn't to build real AI — it was to build enough intuition to understand why real AI can be powerful, and why it can also get things wrong when its training data is biased or too small.
